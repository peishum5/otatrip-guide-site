An izakaya is Japan's version of a pub: you order drinks first, then small plates to share, a few at a time. The food is easy. The unwritten rules are what trip visitors up.

## The Short Version

- **A small dish arrives that you did not order.** It is the *otoshi*, a seating charge of about ¥300–¥500 per person. It is normal.
- **Order a drink first,** then two or three dishes at a time. Everything is shared.
- **Staff do not check on you.** Say *sumimasen* (excuse me) or press the call button.
- **Pay at the register on the way out,** one bill for the table. No tip.
- **Check the last train before the second round.** The last through trains to Osaka leave at 23:15.

## What Happens, Step by Step

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. At the door | Hold up fingers for the number in your group. Wait to be seated. If the floor is raised tatami, shoes come off at the step |
| 2. Hot towel | The *oshibori* is for your hands |
| 3. First drink | Staff ask for drinks before food. Order one each, even if it is tea or a soft drink |
| 4. Otoshi | A small dish arrives without being ordered. It is the seating charge and goes on the bill |
| 5. Food | Order two or three plates to share, then more as you go. There are no courses and no rush |
| 6. More | Call out *sumimasen*, press the button on the table, or use the tablet or QR code menu if there is one |
| 7. Paying | Ask for the bill (*okaikei onegaishimasu*), then pay at the register by the door. One bill per table is the norm |

## Small Rules Worth Knowing

- **Wait for the toast.** Nobody drinks until everyone has a glass and someone says *kanpai*.
- **Pour for others.** With a shared bottle or carafe, fill your neighbor's glass and let them fill yours.
- **Chopsticks:** never stand them upright in rice or pass food from chopsticks to chopsticks. Both belong to funerals.
- **Seats may be timed.** On busy nights many places give you about two hours, and they will tell you when you sit down.
- **All-you-can-drink (*nomihodai*)** is a fixed-price plan with a time limit, usually 60 to 120 minutes, and the whole table has to take it.
- **Cash:** small places are often cash only. Carry enough for the night. See [Cash, ATMs, SIM & Wi-Fi](/blog/plan/money-and-sim).
- **No tipping.** See [Tipping in Japan](/blog/culture/tipping-in-japan).

## Phrases That Do Most of the Work

| Say | Japanese | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Sumimasen | すみません | Excuse me (to call staff) |
| Futari desu | 二人です | Two people |
| Eigo no menyu arimasu ka | 英語のメニューありますか | Do you have an English menu? |
| Nama biiru | 生ビール | Draft beer |
| Osusume wa? | おすすめは？ | What do you recommend? |
| Kore o kudasai | これをください | This one, please (while pointing) |
| Okaikei onegaishimasu | お会計お願いします | The bill, please |

For sake, the first question is dry or sweet. See [how to read a sake menu](/blog/food/sake-kyoto).

## Smoking, Age and the Law

- **Smoking is banned indoors** in principle, and has been since April 2020. Small bars that were already open then can still allow it, and they must show a sign at the door.
- **Nobody under 20 may enter a smoking venue or smoking room,** not even with parents.
- **The drinking age is 20.**
- **Do not cycle home.** Since November 2024, riding a bicycle after drinking can mean up to three years in prison or a ¥500,000 fine, and the person who lent the bicycle can face the same penalty.

<TipBox type="warning" title="IGNORE THE TOUTS">
Street touting is banned by a Kyoto City ordinance in the Gion and Kawaramachi nightlife area, with fines of up to ¥50,000 for the touts. It still happens. If someone on the street offers to take you to a bar or izakaya, keep walking. The places worth going to do not send anyone out to find you.
</TipBox>

## Where to Go in Kyoto

- **Pontocho:** a narrow lantern-lit alley beside the Kamo River, with izakayas at every price. From 1 May to 15 October many open riverside terraces (*kawayuka*). Book ahead on weekends.
- **Kiyamachi:** the street along the Takase canal, one block west. Younger, louder and cheaper.
- **Around Gion-Shijo Station:** casual and lively places, good for sake with grilled food.
- **Teramachi and Sanjo:** everyday izakayas mixed in with ramen and rice-bowl shops.

Vegetarian? It is possible if you ask about the stock. See [Vegetarian & Vegan Kyoto](/blog/food/kyoto-vegetarian).

## Getting Home

| From central Kyoto | Last train on a weekday |
|---|---|
| Keihan, Gion-Shijo to Osaka (Yodoyabashi) | 23:15 |
| Hankyu, Kyoto-Kawaramachi to Osaka-Umeda | 23:15 |
| Subway, Shijo to Kyoto Station | 23:57 |
| JR, Kyoto to Osaka | 23:58 |

Later trains on Keihan and Hankyu stop partway, so check before you sit down for another round. Taxis cost about 20% more between 10pm and 5am.
