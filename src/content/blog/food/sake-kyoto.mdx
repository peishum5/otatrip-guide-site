Almost all Kyoto sake comes from Fushimi, a brewery district in the south of the city that most visitors never reach. Taste there by day and drink in the small bars of Gion and Pontocho at night.

## The Short Version

- **Go to Fushimi for the breweries.** The Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum (¥600, tasting included) and the canal walk fill a half day.
- **Fushimi sake is soft, clean and slightly sweet,** because the local water is very soft.
- **Taste at the breweries, drink at the bars.** The ¥600 Gekkeikan ticket includes three samples. A glass in a bar is ¥500–¥1,500.
- **Can't read the menu?** Ask *karakuchi ka amakuchi ka* (dry or sweet?).
- **For Gion's small bars,** arrive before 6:30pm on a weeknight.

## Fushimi: Where to Go

- **Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum:** Gekkeikan has brewed here since 1637, and the museum fills a preserved Meiji-period warehouse. Exhibits show the full brewing process. ¥600 with three samples, cashless payment only. Open 9:30–16:30, last entry 16:00. A 5-minute walk from Chushojima Station (Keihan Line). Arrive at opening on a weekday and there is no queue at the tasting bar.
- **Kizakura Kappa Country:** a brewery complex with a restaurant, tasting bar and small museum. Free to enter. Walking distance from Fushimi-Momoyama Station.
- **The sake canal:** the old waterway that carried barrels to Osaka, lined with dark-slatted brewery warehouses and willows. Free, and about a 20-minute walk from station to station.

## How to Read a Sake Menu

| Term | On the label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Junmai | 純米 | Pure rice. No added alcohol |
| Ginjo | 吟醸 | Rice polished down to 60% or less. Fragrant, fruity |
| Daiginjo | 大吟醸 | Polished to 50% or less. Delicate, aromatic, expensive |
| Nama | 生 | Unpasteurized. Fresh and fruity |
| Nigori | 濁り | Cloudy and unfiltered. Rich, often slightly sweet |
| Karakuchi / amakuchi | 辛口 / 甘口 | Dry / sweet. The first thing to decide |

- **Fushimi style:** look for *tanrei*, light-bodied and clean.
- **Temperature:** *hiya* (room temperature) shows delicate aromas, *nurukan* (about 40°C) is gently warm, *atsukan* (50°C and up) is hot and bold. Heat improves cheaper sake. Fine daiginjo is better cold.

<TipBox type="warning" title="LABELS ARE NOT IN ENGLISH">
Sake labels and bar menus almost never include English. Point at a bottle and ask <em>karakuchi ka amakuchi ka</em> (辛口か甘口か, dry or sweet?). Staff at most specialist bars can explain the rest in basic English.
</TipBox>

## Where to Drink in the Evening

- **Gion:** small backstreet bars, often with fewer than 15 seats and a knowledgeable owner. Look for *nihonshu-dokoro* (sake specialists). Many take reservations only or turn away walk-ins on Friday and Saturday nights, so go before 6:30pm on a weeknight and sit at the counter.
- **Pontocho:** the narrow alley between Shijo and Sanjo beside the Kamo River, packed with restaurants, bars and sake specialists.
- **Fushimi:** small bars along the canal pour local brews next to the warehouses. More casual, more local, cheaper.
