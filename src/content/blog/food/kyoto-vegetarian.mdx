Kyoto is the easiest city in Japan for vegetarians, but fish stock hides in dishes that look plant-based. Know where to eat and what to ask.

## The Short Version

- **Fish stock (*dashi*) is the real problem.** It is in miso soup, sauces and simmered vegetables. Ask every time.
- **Book one shojin ryori meal.** Buddhist temple cuisine uses no meat, no fish and no fish stock.
- **Tofu and obanzai restaurants are the everyday option.** An obanzai lunch set costs ¥1,200–¥2,500.
- **Ramen is the hardest.** Standard izakayas are possible if you ask about the stock.
- **Show a diet card in Japanese.** It works better than speaking.

<TipBox type="warning" title="THE HIDDEN DASHI PROBLEM">
Dashi is fish stock made from bonito flakes or dried sardines. It is in almost everything that is not clearly labeled plant-based, including dishes that look vegetarian. Strict vegans need to ask at every restaurant.
</TipBox>

## Shojin Ryori

The formal vegetarian cuisine of Zen temples: tofu, *yuba* (tofu skin), *fu* (wheat gluten), sesame, mountain vegetables and pickles. Many kitchens also leave out garlic, onion, green onion, chives and leeks.

- **Tenryu-ji Shigetsu:** inside Tenryu-ji Temple in Arashiyama, with a garden view. Set meals ¥3,800, ¥6,500 or ¥9,000, plus the ¥500 garden admission. Lunch only, closed Thursdays. Fills fast in peak season.
- **Izusen:** near Daitokuji Temple in northern Kyoto. Lunch sets of many small dishes on lacquerware, ¥3,000–¥6,500.
- **Reservations:** book before you arrive in Kyoto. Shigetsu needs two to three days of notice.

## Tofu Restaurants

Kyoto's soft water makes very delicate tofu, and some restaurants serve little else.

- **Tousuiro (central Kyoto):** multi-course tofu kaiseki, from ¥5,000 at lunch. Confirm a fully vegetarian course when you book.
- **Yudofu at Nanzenji:** a cluster of restaurants around Nanzenji Temple serves *yudofu*, tofu simmered in kombu (kelp) stock. Strict vegans should ask what is in the stock and the dipping sauce.
- **Timing:** the Nanzenji restaurants fill between 11:30am and 1pm. Arrive at 11:00, when they open.

## Obanzai

Kyoto home cooking: simmered vegetables, pickles, tofu and whatever is in season.

- **Where:** the Teramachi and Nishiki area has several obanzai lunch spots.
- **Why it works:** buffet-style and set-meal places let you see each dish before you choose.
- **Cost:** ¥1,200–¥2,500 for rice, miso soup and several small vegetable dishes.
- **Watch for:** dashi in the broths and the miso soup. Ask.

## Phrases That Work

| Situation | Japanese | Romaji |
|---|---|---|
| I'm vegetarian | 私はベジタリアンです | Watashi wa bejitarian desu |
| No meat or fish | 肉と魚は食べられません | Niku to sakana wa taberaremasen |
| Is there fish stock? | だしは魚ですか？ | Dashi wa sakana desu ka? |
| No egg or dairy | 卵と乳製品も食べられません | Tamago to nyuuseihin mo taberaremasen |
| Vegan | ヴィーガン | Viigan |

A written card beats a spoken phrase. Screenshot a Japanese dietary card before your trip, and use the Google Translate camera to read menus.
