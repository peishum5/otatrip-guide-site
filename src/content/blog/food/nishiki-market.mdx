Nishiki Market is a 390-metre covered arcade of about 120 shops between Teramachi and Takakura streets. Some stalls are excellent and some now exist for tourists, so what you skip matters as much as what you try.

## The Short Version

- **Go before 11am on a weekday.** By early afternoon the narrow arcade is packed.
- **Snack here, eat lunch elsewhere.** Allow 60–90 minutes.
- **Best buys:** pickles, fresh tofu, tamagoyaki and warabi-mochi.
- **Skip** the trinket shops and the sweets made for Instagram.
- **Eat at the stall where you bought it,** not while walking.

## What to Eat

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/nishiki-market-arcade.jpg" alt="Nishiki Market covered arcade with colorful stall banners and shoppers" badge="NISHIKI ICHIBA" caption="Walking the full 390 metres takes 15–20 minutes without stopping." />

| Food | What it is | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Tamagoyaki** | Sweet rolled omelet on a skewer, served warm. Pick a stall that is cooking it fresh on a rectangular pan | ¥200–¥300 |
| **Dashimaki tamago** | The thick, savory rolled egg, flavored with dashi | varies |
| **Tofu donuts** | Small, freshly fried and lightly sweet. Eat them at once: they lose their texture in minutes | ¥150–¥200 each |
| **Tsukemono (pickles)** | *Shibazuke* (purple eggplant and shiso), *senmaizuke* (thin-sliced turnip), *suguki* (fermented turnip). Many shops offer samples | ¥600–¥1,200 a bag |
| **Fresh tofu** | Small tastes of silky Kyoto tofu at the tofu shops, some with a little dashi sauce | free–¥200 |
| **Grilled seafood skewers** | Octopus, squid and shellfish grilled to order. Choose a stall using charcoal, not gas | varies |
| **Warabi-mochi** | Soft, translucent dumplings with roasted soybean flour and black sugar syrup, sold by weight. Buy where it is made fresh | varies |
| **Matcha sweets and wagashi** | Quality varies a lot. Good shops change their items by season and keep a plain, traditional display | varies |

Vacuum-packed pickles keep for weeks and travel well. They are the best-value souvenir in the market.

## What to Skip

- **Souvenir shops:** chopsticks, key rings and mass-produced "Kyoto goods" have nothing to do with a food market.
- **Photo-first sweets:** fruit parfaits and matcha-everything stalls. Check the line. Teenagers with phones, or local shoppers?
- **A full lunch:** Nishiki is for snacks before or after a meal somewhere else.
- **Which end:** the eastern end near Teramachi has turned most toward tourists. The western half toward Takakura keeps more of the original pickle, tofu and dry goods shops, so start there.
