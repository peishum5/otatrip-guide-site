Himeji Castle is the one Japanese castle that is genuinely original — never burned, never bombed, never rebuilt in concrete — and it sits 55 minutes from Kyoto by Shinkansen. It is also, since March 2026, two and a half times more expensive to enter than it was. This guide covers how to get there, what the day actually costs now, and how to decide whether it earns a slot in your trip.

## The 2026 Price Change

Until early 2026, Himeji Castle cost ¥1,000 to enter. From **March 2026 it is ¥2,500** for general adult visitors — a 150% increase, and one of the steepest at any major Japanese sight.

Two things soften it. **Under-18s now enter free**, regardless of where they live, which makes this materially cheaper for families than it used to be. And residents of Himeji pay a reduced rate, verified by My Number card — a two-tier system that overseas visitors cannot access, and which is worth knowing about so the ticket window is not a surprise.

<TipBox type="budget" title="WHAT THE DAY ACTUALLY COSTS NOW">
For two adults from Kyoto on the Shinkansen: about ¥18,960 in train fares plus ¥5,000 in entry — roughly ¥24,000 before lunch. Taking the JR Special Rapid instead cuts the transport to ¥8,840 and brings the day down to around ¥15,000 for the extra 70 minutes of travel. If the budget is tight, the Special Rapid is the difference between "expensive" and "prohibitive".
</TipBox>

## Getting There

| Option | Time | Fare (one way) | JR Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shinkansen (Hikari / Kodama)** | ~55 min | ~¥4,740 non-reserved | Covered |
| **JR Special Rapid** *(best value)* | ~90 min | ¥2,210 | Covered |

**Shinkansen.** Hikari and Kodama services run direct from Kyoto to Himeji — no transfer at Shin-Osaka needed, which several guides get wrong. Roughly ¥4,740 non-reserved, made up of a ¥2,210 base fare and a ¥2,530 limited express surcharge. Note that not every Hikari stops at Himeji, so check the board.

**JR Special Rapid (新快速).** The same ¥2,210 base fare with no surcharge, direct from Kyoto Station in about 90 minutes. No reservation, frequent departures, and comfortable enough — this is what people in Kansai actually take. Unless your day is tightly scheduled, the ¥2,530 saved each way is better spent on lunch.

**JR Pass holders:** both are covered. Take the Shinkansen.

## At the Castle

Himeji Station's north exit opens onto a wide avenue with the castle framed at the end of it. The walk is about 20 minutes, flat and pleasant. A loop bus also runs if you would rather not.

**Why it matters.** Most Japanese castles you can visit are postwar concrete reconstructions with lifts inside. Himeji is not: the keep dates from 1609, survived the bombing of Himeji in 1945 and the 1995 Kobe earthquake, and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and National Treasure. You climb original wooden stairs through six floors of a defensive structure built to be fought in — gun racks, arrow slits, and stone-drop chutes all still in place.

**Allow 1.5–2 hours.** The approach spirals through a series of gates and courtyards designed to confuse attackers, which takes longer than the map suggests, and the keep itself involves steep wooden staircases in stockinged feet.

<TipBox type="warning" title="ARRIVE AT 9AM, NOT AFTER LUNCH">
The castle opens at 9:00 and the buildings start closing from 16:30. Entry to the main keep is capped on busy days, and the queue is the whole experience if you time it badly. On a day trip from Kyoto this means taking a train that gets you in before 10am — which is the single biggest thing you can control.
</TipBox>

<TipBox type="local" title="ADD KOKO-EN IF YOU HAVE TIME">
Koko-en, next to the castle, is a set of nine walled Edo-style gardens built on the former samurai residences. A combined castle and garden ticket runs about ¥2,600 — effectively ¥100 for the gardens, which is the best-value add-on in the city. It is also considerably quieter than the castle.
</TipBox>

## Should You Go?

**Go if** you have three or more days in Kyoto and a real interest in castles, military architecture, or history. There is nothing like it in Kyoto, and photographs undersell the scale.

**Skip it if** this is a short trip and your Kyoto list is still unfinished. Himeji is one building, six hours round trip, and now ¥2,500 to enter. [Nara](/blog/day-trips/nara-from-kyoto) offers more variety at a third of the travel time, and [Osaka](/blog/day-trips/osaka-from-kyoto) is 30 minutes away with a completely different atmosphere.

**A compromise:** Himeji sits on the line between Kyoto and Hiroshima. If you are travelling onward west, stop at Himeji for three hours with your bags in a station locker rather than making a dedicated round trip from Kyoto.
