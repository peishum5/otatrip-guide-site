Uji is 17 minutes from Kyoto Station. It gives you two things the Kyoto temple circuit does not: matcha at its source, and a World Heritage hall you can photograph without a crowd in the frame.

## The Short Version

- **The shortest worthwhile trip from Kyoto.** Half a day (3–4 hours) is enough.
- **Take the JR Nara Line:** 17 minutes, ¥240, covered by the JR Pass.
- **Buy the Phoenix Hall interior ticket the moment you arrive.** It is ¥300 extra, 50 people per slot, and sells out early on busy days.
- **Drink your matcha here.** A seated set with sweets is ¥1,200–1,800, and most "Kyoto matcha" is grown in Uji anyway.
- **It stays quiet.** Even on peak weekends Uji is calmer than Arashiyama or Fushimi Inari.

## Getting There

| Option | Route | Time | Fare | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **JR Nara Line** *(simplest)* | Kyoto Station → Uji | 17 min | ¥240 | JR Pass valid |
| Keihan | Gion-Shijo → change at Chushojima → Keihan-Uji | about 35 min | ¥430 | Handy from Gion or Fushimi. Arrives nearer Ujigami Shrine |

Both stations are within a 10-minute walk of Byodoin.

## Byodoin Temple

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/uji-byodoin-pond-reflection.jpg" alt="Byodoin Phoenix Hall reflected in the central pond on a calm morning" badge="BYODOIN" caption="The Phoenix Hall mirrored in its pond. Calm, clear mornings give the cleanest reflection." />

- **The Phoenix Hall:** built in 1053, the building on the back of the ¥10 coin. It seems to float on its pond, with two bronze phoenixes on the roof.
- **Entry:** ¥1,000 for the garden and the Hoshokan museum.
- **Inside the hall:** a separate timed ticket (¥300) gets you a short guided viewing of the Amida Buddha statue by the sculptor Jocho, a National Treasure.
- **Hoshokan museum:** holds the original bronze phoenixes and temple bells. Included in the entry fee.

<TipBox type="warning" title="INTERIOR TICKETS SELL OUT FAST">
Only 50 people per time slot can enter the Phoenix Hall. Buy the ¥300 interior ticket before you do anything else. On busy days the slots fill within the first hour after opening.
</TipBox>

## Matcha

Uji leaf is the reference standard for matcha, and buying here is often cheaper than buying the same grade in Kyoto.

- **Nakamura Tokichi:** the most famous tea house in Uji, open since 1854. The main shop (Honten) is near JR Uji Station and a second branch sits by the river near Byodoin. Matcha sets, parfaits and soft serve. Expect a queue on weekends.
- **Tsuen:** claims to be Japan's oldest tea shop, founded around 1160. It stands at the east end of Uji Bridge. Simple matcha sets and seasonal sweets.
- **Marukyu Koyamaen:** a premium producer with clear labelling. A small tin of ceremonial-grade matcha is the best souvenir in town.

## Ujigami Shrine

A 10-minute walk from Byodoin, across the river by the small Asagiri Bridge. Its main hall dates to the late Heian period and is the oldest surviving shrine building in Japan. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, free to enter, wooded and almost empty even in peak season.

## Half-Day Route

1. **09:30 JR Uji Station.** Walk toward the river. The approach street to Byodoin is lined with tea shops. Save them for later.
2. **10:00 Byodoin.** 90 minutes. Interior ticket first, then the garden, then the museum.
3. **11:30 Riverside and matcha.** 60 minutes. Walk the river path, then sit down for a matcha set at Nakamura Tokichi.
4. **12:30 Ujigami Shrine.** 30 minutes. Cross Asagiri Bridge and come back the same way.
5. **13:30 Tea shopping.** 30 minutes on the approach street, ¥500–3,000. Look in at Tsuen by the bridge.
6. **14:00 JR back to Kyoto.** 17 minutes.
