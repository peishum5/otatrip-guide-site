Nara was Japan's capital before Kyoto. Its Great Buddha, a UNESCO-listed shrine and about 1,700 free-roaming deer all sit within walking distance of each other, 35 minutes from Kyoto Station.

## The Short Version

- **The easiest half-day trip from Kyoto.** Three to four hours covers Todai-ji, the deer and Kasuga Shrine.
- **Take the Kintetsu Express:** 45–50 minutes, ¥760, no reservation. The Limited Express saves 10 minutes for ¥520 more.
- **JR Pass holders take the JR Nara Line rapid:** 45 minutes and covered by the pass.
- **Arrive before 10am on a weekday.** By midday the Todai-ji approach is packed.
- **Keep food hidden from the deer.** They are wild and they push.

## Getting There

| Option | Arrives at | Time | Fare | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kintetsu Express (急行)** *(best value)* | Kintetsu-Nara | 45–50 min | ¥760 | No reservation. What most locals take |
| **Kintetsu Limited Express** | Kintetsu-Nara | 35 min | ¥1,280 | ¥760 fare plus ¥520 reserved seat |
| JR Nara Line (rapid) | JR Nara | 45 min | ¥720 | JR Pass valid. Local trains take 75 min |

Kintetsu trains leave from Kintetsu Kyoto Station, on the south side of Kyoto Station next to the Shinkansen gates. **Kintetsu-Nara Station** puts you in the centre, a short walk from the park. JR Nara Station is about 15 minutes further on foot.

## What to See

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/nara-deer-park.jpg" alt="Sika deer bowing to a visitor holding shika senbei crackers in Nara Park" badge="NARA PARK" caption="The deer have learned to bow for crackers. A bundle costs ¥200 from vendors across the park." />

- **Nara Park and the deer:** about 1,700 sika deer roam the park, treated as sacred messengers of Kasuga Shrine. Buy *shika senbei* crackers (¥200) and they will bow for them.
- **Todai-ji:** home of the Daibutsu, a 15-metre bronze Buddha and the largest in Japan. The hall around it is one of the largest wooden buildings on earth, yet only two-thirds the width of the 8th-century original. Entry ¥800 (¥400 for ages 6–12). Look for the pillar with a hole in its base: squeezing through is said to bring good luck.
- **Kasuga Taisha:** a 20-minute walk through the park from Todai-ji. Founded in 768 and UNESCO listed. The forest approach is lined with stone lanterns. Outer grounds are free, the inner sanctuary is ¥700. All the lanterns are lit twice a year, at Setsubun in early February and Obon in mid-August.
- **Naramachi:** the old merchant district south of the park, now cafes and craft shops in wooden townhouses. Eat lunch here, not at the overpriced places near Todai-ji.

<TipBox type="warning" title="DEER SAFETY">
The deer are wild animals. They push and nip when they smell food, so keep snack bags closed and out of sight. Bucks can be aggressive in the autumn rut (October–November). Never corner a deer, and keep small children close.
</TipBox>

## Half-Day Route

1. **09:30 Kintetsu-Nara Station.** Walk northeast toward the park. The deer appear within minutes.
2. **10:00 Todai-ji.** 60 minutes, ¥800. Pause at the Nandaimon gate and its giant guardian statues on the way in.
3. **11:15 Nara Park.** 45 minutes with the deer as you walk south.
4. **12:00 Kasuga Taisha.** 30–45 minutes. The forest approach alone is worth it.
5. **13:00 Naramachi.** A 10-minute walk south. Lunch for ¥1,000–¥1,500.
6. **14:30 Train back.** The Limited Express has you in Kyoto around 3:10pm.

For a full day, add Horyu-ji, home of the world's oldest wooden buildings. Take the JR Yamatoji Line from JR Nara to Horyuji Station (about 12 minutes), then walk 20 minutes or take the short bus.
