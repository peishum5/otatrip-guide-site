Gion is a working entertainment district that most visitors see as a 20-minute walk down one crowded street. Come in the evening, know four streets, and follow one rule.

## The Short Version

- **Go on a weekday evening, 5pm–8pm.** Midday belongs to tour groups.
- **Walk four streets:** Hanamikoji, the Shirakawa canal at Shimbashi, Ishibei-koji and Nene-no-michi.
- **Seeing a maiko is luck.** Your best odds are 5:30–6:30pm on a weekday on Hanamikoji.
- **The rule:** never block, follow or photograph maiko and geisha, and stay out of private alleys.

## The Four Streets

- **[Hanamikoji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Hanamikoji+Street+Kyoto):** the classic row of *ochaya*, the private teahouses where geisha entertain. Walk the part south of Shijo Street. North of Shijo it is modern bars and restaurants.
- **[Shimbashi and the Shirakawa canal](https://maps.google.com/?q=Gion+Shirakawa+Kyoto):** a stone lane beside the water under willow trees. Quieter than Hanamikoji and, for many people, more beautiful.
- **[Ishibei-koji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ishibei-koji+Kyoto):** a short stone-paved lane between high walls. No shops, almost no people. Photography is banned here, so put the camera away and just walk it.
- **[Nene-no-michi](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nene-no-Michi+Kyoto):** the stone path behind Kodaiji Temple that links Gion to the [Higashiyama walk](/blog/neighborhoods/higashiyama).

Maiko are apprentices in bright kimono and elaborate hair ornaments. Geisha, called *geiko* in Kyoto, wear simpler and darker kimono. Both walk fast to evening appointments, so stand against a wall on Hanamikoji, stay quiet and wait. Some evenings you see three in an hour, some evenings none.

In July the whole district becomes the center of the [Gion Matsuri](/blog/seasonal/gion-matsuri).

<TipBox type="warning" title="THE RULE THAT KEEPS GION OPEN">
Do not approach, follow, call out to or block maiko and geisha, and do not photograph them without clear consent. They are professionals on their way to work.<br /><br />
Private side alleys off Hanamikoji are closed to visitors, and the signs warn of a ¥10,000 fine. Stay on the main street.
</TipBox>

## Eating and Drinking

- **Kaiseki on Hanamikoji:** the high end. Book weeks ahead.
- **Izakayas near Gion-Shijo Station:** casual and lively, good for sake with grilled food.
- **Pontocho:** one block west across the river, a narrow alley with restaurants at every price. Walk it once to read the menus, then choose. Book ahead on weekends.
- **[Nishiki Market](/blog/food/nishiki-market):** a 10-minute walk west, best in the daytime.
