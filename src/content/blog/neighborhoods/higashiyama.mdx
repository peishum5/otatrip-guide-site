Higashiyama is the walk that makes people fall for Kyoto: about 3 km of stone lanes and wooden townhouses from Kiyomizudera down to Yasaka Shrine. Start early and walk it south to north.

## The Short Version

- **Walk south to north.** Start at Kiyomizudera and finish at Yasaka Shrine, so the busiest stop is behind you as the crowds build.
- **Start early.** Kiyomizudera opens at 6am, and the lanes are nearly empty before 9am.
- **Allow 2–3 hours** for the 3 km.
- **Two entry fees:** Kiyomizudera ¥500 and Kodaiji ¥600. The lanes, the park and the shrine are free.
- **Wear flat rubber soles.** The stone slopes are steep, uneven and slippery when wet.

## The Route in Order

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/higashiyama-sannenzaka-morning.jpg" alt="Sannenzaka stone-paved lane in early morning with traditional machiya townhouses on both sides" badge="SANNENZAKA" caption="Early morning on Sannenzaka. The stone path is steep and slightly uneven." />

1. **[Kiyomizudera](/blog/sights/kiyomizudera):** the hilltop temple with the famous wooden stage. Allow 60–75 minutes. ¥500.
2. **Sannenzaka:** walk down Kiyomizuzaka lane and turn onto this steep stone slope lined with old shopfronts. Local legend says a fall here brings three years of bad luck, so take it slowly.
3. **Ninenzaka:** a shorter, gentler slope heading north, with ceramics, lacquerware, matcha and yatsuhashi sweets. The older shops with worn signs usually sell the better goods.
4. **Ishibei-koji:** a short detour between Ninenzaka and Kodaiji. Stone paving, high walls, lanterns, no shops and very few people. The entrance is a small gap that is easy to walk past, with no sign from the main path. Photography is banned in the lane.
5. **Kodaiji:** founded in 1606 by Nene, the wife of Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Fine lacquerwork and a small bamboo grove. ¥600. The evening garden light-ups in spring (late March to May) and autumn (mid-October to December) are among Kyoto's best.
6. **Maruyama Park:** free and always open. Its weeping cherry tree is lit at night in blossom season.
7. **Yasaka Shrine:** free and always open. The main gate opens straight onto Shijo Avenue and Gion.

## Getting There and Away

- **To the start:** City Bus 206 from Kyoto Station to Gojo-zaka or Kiyomizu-michi, then about 10 minutes uphill on foot.
- **From the end:** Gion-Shijo Station (Keihan) is a 5-minute walk west of Yasaka Shrine, and Kyoto-Kawaramachi Station (Hankyu) is 10 minutes.

Peak season means cherry blossom and autumn leaves. On those weekends, go before 10am.

<TipBox type="warning" title="WATCH YOUR FOOTING">
Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka are paved with irregular stone slabs that turn slippery in the rain. Heels, smooth dress shoes and flip-flops are a real hazard, most of all on the steep Sannenzaka descent.
</TipBox>

## Eating on the Route

The small teahouses on Ninenzaka that serve matcha and mochi are worth a stop. Pick one that looks local, sit down and take the break.
