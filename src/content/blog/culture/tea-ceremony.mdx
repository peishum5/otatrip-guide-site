A tea ceremony in Kyoto can be a real hour of *temae*, the host's formal preparation of the tea, or a 20-minute bowl of matcha in a rented room. The listings rarely say which one you are booking.

## The Short Version

- **Check the length.** A session with a full demonstration and explanation runs 45–60 minutes. At 20–30 minutes you get matcha and a sweet.
- **Expect ¥3,000–¥5,000** for a standard experience.
- **Choose small groups, under 8 guests,** and a host trained with a recognized tea school. Price alone tells you little.
- **Book as soon as your dates are fixed** if you come in cherry blossom or autumn season.
- **You need no experience.** Eat the sweet first, take the bowl in both hands, turn it clockwise, then drink.

## What Each Type Includes

- **Basic:** matcha and a *wagashi* sweet. No demonstration.
- **Standard:** temae demonstration, explanation, matcha and a sweet.
- **Private formal:** the full ceremony in a private room with a teacher.
- **Multi-day lessons:** from ¥10,000.

A well-run ¥4,000 session can feel more genuine than a rushed ¥8,000 package that packs in too many activities.

## Where to Book

- **Tea Ceremony Camellia (Higashiyama):** in the Ninenzaka area. Shared 45-minute sessions of up to 8 guests cost ¥4,000, or ¥5,000 in peak months. Private sessions start at ¥8,000 per person. Kimono rental can be added for ¥6,000.
- **Tea Ceremony Koto (Kinkakuji):** a one-minute walk from the Golden Pavilion. Shared 45-minute sessions cost ¥3,300. Private sessions start at ¥7,700 per person.
- **Temple matcha:** not a ceremony, but a bowl of matcha with a garden view for roughly ¥600–¥1,000, usually on top of the temple admission. Kodaiji and Nanzenji are two places to ask.

## What Happens in the Room

1. **Shoes off, sit on tatami.** The room is small, traditionally 4.5 mats (about 7 m²). The scroll and flowers in the alcove change with the season.
2. **The host prepares the tea in silence.** Every movement has a purpose. You do not need to understand it all, just watch.
3. **The sweet comes first.** Eat it before the tea. It balances the bitterness.
4. **The bowl:** take it in both hands, turn it clockwise twice so you do not drink from the decorated front, drink in three and a half sips, then wipe the rim with your finger.

Most tourist sessions serve *usucha*, thin tea whisked until frothy. *Koicha*, a thick and intense paste, appears in longer and more formal sessions. Drink it slowly.

The best venues in Gion and Higashiyama fill weeks in advance for cherry blossom (late March to early April) and autumn foliage (mid-November). Walk-ins are rarely possible at the quality venues.
</TipBox>

## Manners That Matter

- **Arrive on time.** A late arrival disrupts the whole group.
- **Wear no perfume.** It covers the scent of the tea.
- **Do not worry about getting it perfect.** Hosts of tourist sessions are patient.
