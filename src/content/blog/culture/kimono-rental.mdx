Renting a kimono is the most common way visitors dress up in Kyoto, and a ¥3,000 rental is not the same thing as a ¥15,000 one. Know what each price includes, book ahead, and rent next to where you want to walk.

## The Short Version

- **Basic packages start around ¥3,000** and cover the kimono, obi and dressing. Hair styling and accessories come with the ¥5,000–¥8,000 tier.
- **Book online 3–7 days ahead.** Walk-ins only work on quiet weekdays.
- **Rent where you want to walk.** Higashiyama has the most shops, Arashiyama the fewest.
- **Allow 20–40 minutes for dressing** and plan to return by 5–6pm.
- **Almost all rental kimono are synthetic, not silk.** They look nearly identical in photos and are easier to walk in.

## What Each Price Includes

- **Basic:** kimono, obi, dressing, *tabi* socks and *zori* sandals.
- **Standard:** all of that plus hair styling and accessories.
- **Premium:** silk kimono, full styling and photos. Worth it only for serious portraits or if you want to feel real silk.
- **Hair styling on its own:** usually ¥500–¥2,750 extra and 15–30 minutes. Arrive with a simple updo and you save both. Most shops add hair ornaments anyway.
- **Yukata:** the lighter cotton version for June to September. Cheaper and easier to wear.
- **Rain:** a rain option is usually included, and shops often lend an umbrella.

## Where to Rent

- **[Higashiyama](/blog/neighborhoods/higashiyama) and Kiyomizudera:** the most shops in Kyoto, along the approach to Kiyomizudera and on Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka. You step out of the shop straight onto the stone lanes.
  - **Okamoto:** long-established, with several shops around Kiyomizudera. Plans from about ¥3,300, formal kimono from ¥11,000.
- **[Gion](/blog/neighborhoods/gion):** fewer shops, but the best match for walking Hanamikoji and the Shimbashi canal.
  - **Kyoto Kimono Rental Wargo:** shops in Gion and in Kyoto Tower by Kyoto Station. Standard plan from ¥3,300 when booked online.
- **Gojo (central Kyoto):** away from the sights, but large shops with a wide choice.
  - **Yumeyakata:** one of the largest, a 3-minute walk from Gojo subway station and 10 minutes from Keihan Kiyomizu-Gojo. Plans from ¥4,180, or ¥6,600 with hair styling.
- **Arashiyama:** a handful of shops near Togetsukyo Bridge for walking the bamboo grove.

<TipBox type="warning" title="BOOK BEFORE YOU ARRIVE">
In cherry blossom season (late March to April) and autumn foliage season (November), shops fill up days in advance.<br /><br />
Arashiyama has far fewer shops than Higashiyama. Walk-ins there are nearly impossible on weekends and in peak season, so book before the day, not that morning.
</TipBox>

## On the Day

1. **Check in** at your booked time. Many shops run in waves (9am, 10am and so on).
2. **Choose** your kimono and obi. Let the staff suggest combinations. Their advice is good.
3. **Get dressed.** 20–40 minutes, plus 15–30 minutes if hair styling is included.
4. **Walk.** Return by closing time, usually 5–6pm. Some shops offer overnight rental for ryokan stays.

## Wearing It All Day

- **Steps:** small ones. You will move more slowly than usual.
- **Stairs:** temples and shrines have stone staircases. Hold the hem when you climb.
- **Sandals:** *zori* are worn with split-toed socks, and your heel hangs slightly off the back.
- **Obi:** it is tied snugly. Some people find it tight over a long day.
- **Heavy rain:** a real problem, because the fabric soaks up water. Some shops let you cancel or reschedule.
