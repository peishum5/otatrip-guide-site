Kyoto is the part of Japan where cash still matters: temple gates, market stalls and small restaurants often take nothing else. Sort out cash and mobile data before you land and you will not think about either again.

## The Short Version

- **Carry ¥10,000–¥20,000 in cash.** Cards work at hotels and chains, not at small temples and stalls.
- **Withdraw at 7-Eleven.** Open 24 hours, in English, and it takes almost every foreign card. Skip ordinary bank ATMs.
- **Buy an eSIM before you fly** and activate it on Wi-Fi at home.
- **In a group of three or more,** one pocket Wi-Fi can make more sense than separate plans.
- **Bring a power bank.** Maps and translation drain a phone before dinner.

## Cards or Cash

**Cards that work**

- **Visa and Mastercard:** most hotels, department stores, convenience stores and chain restaurants.
- **American Express:** upscale restaurants and big department stores such as Isetan and Takashimaya, but not everywhere.
- **Discover:** rarely accepted. Do not rely on it.

**Where you need cash**

- Small shrine and temple gates. Entry is often ¥300–¥600, cash only, and a temple day can mean five or six of them.
- Neighborhood restaurants and ramen shops.
- [Nishiki Market](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nishiki+Market+Kyoto) stalls and street food.
- Some traditional ryokan and guesthouses, which want the full bill in cash at checkout.
- Some taxis, though the larger fleets now take cards.

Withdraw before you head out into the neighborhoods, not after.

## ATMs That Take Foreign Cards

Ordinary Japanese bank ATMs (Mizuho, SMBC, regional banks) often reject foreign cards. Use these instead.

| ATM | Where | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **7-Eleven** | Inside every 7-Eleven | 24 hours, full English menu, takes Visa, Mastercard, Maestro and Plus cards. Your default |
| **Japan Post** | Post offices. Look for the red and white JP logo | Very reliable, but closes in the evening and earlier on weekends |
| **FamilyMart** | Inside FamilyMart stores | 24 hours, works well with Visa and Mastercard |
| **AEON Bank** | [AEON Mall](https://maps.google.com/?q=AEON+Mall+Kyoto) near [Kyoto Station](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kyoto+Station+Japan) | Strong foreign-card support |

**Fees:** ¥110–¥220 per withdrawal on the Japanese side, plus your own bank's charge. Take out one larger amount instead of several small ones.

## Mobile Data

| Option | Cost | Best for | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **eSIM** | About $5–$10 for 1 GB, $25–$30 for 20 GB over 30 days | One or two travelers | Needs an unlocked phone that supports eSIM |
| **Physical SIM** | ¥2,000–¥4,000 for 15–50 GB over 10–30 days | Phones without eSIM | Data only, no voice |
| **Pocket Wi-Fi** | Rental, cheaper booked online ahead | Groups of three or more | Battery lasts 6–8 hours, and when it dies everyone is offline |

**Setting up an eSIM**

1. Buy the plan online before you leave. Airalo is the most widely used app.
2. Scan the QR code in your phone's eSIM settings (on iPhone: Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM) and label it "Japan Data".
3. On landing, switch mobile data to the eSIM. Your home SIM stays active for calls and verification texts.

Most recent phones, including iPhone XS and later, can run a home SIM and a Japan eSIM at the same time.

<TipBox type="warning" title="DO THESE TWO THINGS BEFORE YOU FLY">
<strong>Activate the eSIM on Wi-Fi at home or at the departure airport.</strong> It needs a connection to install, and you will not have one when you land.<br /><br />
<strong>Check your phone is not carrier-locked.</strong> A phone still on a carrier payment plan may refuse another provider's eSIM. On iPhone, look at Settings > General > About > Carrier Lock.
</TipBox>

**Where to buy a physical SIM:** counters in the arrivals halls at Kansai (KIX), Osaka Itami and Narita, or [Yodobashi Camera](https://maps.google.com/?q=Yodobashi+Camera+Kyoto) near Kyoto Station and BIC Camera, where staff can help with setup.

**Renting pocket Wi-Fi:** pick up at an airport counter (Ninja WiFi, Global Advanced Communications, Wi-Ho) or have it sent to your hotel. Confirm how you return it, because a missed return costs a penalty fee.

## Small Things That Save the Day

- **Power bank:** a 10,000 mAh unit recharges most phones at least twice. Charge it every night.
- **Offline maps:** in Google Maps, search Kyoto, open the three-dot menu and choose "Download offline map". Signal is weak on some temple mountain paths and in underground stations.
- **IC cards are separate from your data plan.** You load a Suica or ICOCA with money at station machines. See [How to Get Around Kyoto](/blog/plan/transport-kyoto).
- **Do not count on café Wi-Fi.** It exists, but it is slow and usually asks you to register.
