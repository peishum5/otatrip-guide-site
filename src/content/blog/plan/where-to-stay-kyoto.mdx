Four areas work for a first visit to Kyoto. The choice is mostly atmosphere against convenience.

## The Short Version

- **Gion / Higashiyama for atmosphere.** You get the lantern-lit lanes before and after the day crowds. It costs more and has stairs and slopes.
- **Downtown (Kawaramachi / Karasuma) for convenience.** The best food, and transit in every direction.
- **Kyoto Station for day trips and budget.** The widest choice of hotels and the least charm.
- **Arashiyama for a quiet ryokan night.** Beautiful, but 30–50 minutes from everything else.
- **Book three to six months ahead** for cherry blossom and autumn leaf weeks.

## The Four Areas Compared

| Area | Why stay | Watch out for | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gion / Higashiyama** | Historic lanes, evening walks, ryokan | Crowds until 6pm, steep stairs, hard with a wheelchair | Couples, culture lovers, anyone after the "Kyoto feeling" |
| **Downtown** | Best dining, transit hub, modern comfort | Busy, noisy streets | Food lovers, shoppers, families |
| **Kyoto Station** | Most hotels, JR and Shinkansen, easy arrival | Little charm, 15–20 min by bus to the main sights | Day trips to Nara and Osaka, tight budgets |
| **Arashiyama** | River views, nature, top ryokan | 30–50 min to central sights, few places to eat | Slow travelers, repeat visitors, couples |

## Gion & Higashiyama

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/where-to-stay-gion-lanterns.jpg" alt="Gion lanterns glowing at dusk along stone-paved Hanamikoji street" badge="GION" caption="Hanamikoji after the crowds leave. Seeing it like this is the main reason to sleep in the neighborhood." />

- **Rooms:** ryokan at ¥15,000–¥50,000 per person, usually with dinner and breakfast. Machiya townhouses for 2–8 guests at ¥20,000–¥60,000 for the whole house. Boutique hotels and small inns at ¥12,000–¥25,000 per room.
- **Walk to:** [Kiyomizu-dera](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kiyomizudera+Kyoto), [Yasaka Shrine](https://maps.google.com/?q=Yasaka+Shrine+Kyoto), [Chion-in](https://maps.google.com/?q=Chion-in+Kyoto), [Shoren-in](https://maps.google.com/?q=Shoren-in+Kyoto) and [Maruyama Park](https://maps.google.com/?q=Maruyama+Park+Kyoto), all within 15–20 minutes.
- **Know:** near Shijo Street, weekend noise can last until midnight. Stone steps and cobbles make wheelchairs and strollers difficult.

## Downtown (Kawaramachi / Karasuma)

The area between Kawaramachi and Karasuma stations, from Shijo Street north to Oike Street. If Gion is about atmosphere, downtown is about efficiency.

- **Rooms:** business and chain hotels at ¥8,000–¥20,000 per room, breakfast usually extra. Hostels and guesthouses from ¥3,000–¥5,000 per person. The best room size for the price in Kyoto, which suits families.
- **Walk to:** [Nishiki Market](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nishiki+Market+Kyoto), the Teramachi and Shinkyogoku arcades, and [Pontocho](https://maps.google.com/?q=Pontocho+Kyoto) alley.
- **Know:** the subway Karasuma Line and the Keihan Line are both close, so you rarely need a bus. It is the busiest part of the city and feels urban.

## Kyoto Station Area

[Kyoto Station](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kyoto+Station+Japan) is not glamorous, but it works: Shinkansen, JR, subway and most bus routes in one building, with department stores and restaurants inside.

- **Rooms:** the widest range in Kyoto, from business hotels at ¥6,000 to international hotels at ¥25,000 and up, many within a 5-minute walk of the station.
- **Day trips:** [Nara](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nara+Japan) is 35–45 minutes by train. [Osaka](https://maps.google.com/?q=Osaka+Japan) is 15 minutes by Shinkansen or about 30 by JR rapid train. Fushimi Inari is two stops south by JR.
- **Walk to:** [Toji Temple](https://maps.google.com/?q=Toji+Temple+Kyoto) and its five-story pagoda. The rest of central Kyoto is 15–20 minutes by bus or 10–15 by subway.
- **A common plan:** stay here on your first and last nights for easy arrivals and departures, and in Gion or Arashiyama in between.

## Arashiyama

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/where-to-stay-arashiyama-river.jpg" alt="Arashiyama riverside ryokan with forested mountain backdrop and calm Oi River in early morning mist" badge="ARASHIYAMA" caption="A riverside ryokan in Arashiyama, at its best before the day-trippers arrive." />

- **Rooms:** mostly high-end ryokan with river or mountain views at ¥20,000–¥80,000 per person, dinner and breakfast included. A few guesthouses and mid-range hotels at ¥8,000–¥15,000.
- **Walk to:** the bamboo grove, [Tenryu-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Tenryu-ji+Kyoto), Jojakko-ji, Okochi Sanso villa and the riverside. You can be in the grove before 8am, while it is still quiet.
- **Know:** central Kyoto is 30–50 minutes away by train (JR Sagano Line to Kyoto Station, or Hankyu to Kyoto-Kawaramachi). Stay here for the days you plan to spend in the west of the city.

## Booking Tips

- **Book early for April and November.** Ryokan and machiya go first. Station-area hotels last longer but climb in price.
- **Use luggage forwarding.** A hotel front desk or convenience store sends your suitcase to your next hotel by the following day, which makes a machiya with no elevator manageable. See [Luggage in Kyoto](/blog/plan/kyoto-luggage-storage) for costs and cut-off times.
- **Check transit from your area** in [How to Get Around Kyoto](/blog/plan/transport-kyoto).

<TipBox type="warning" title="RYOKAN RULES THAT CATCH PEOPLE OUT">
<strong>Prices are per person, not per room.</strong> A ¥25,000 quote means ¥50,000 a night for a couple. It usually includes a kaiseki dinner and breakfast, so compare it with a hotel plus a restaurant meal.<br /><br />
<strong>Cancellation is strict.</strong> In peak season, expect a 50–100% charge within 7–30 days of arrival.<br /><br />
<strong>River-view rooms in Arashiyama sell out</strong> within hours of opening for cherry blossom and mid-November dates. Check as soon as your dates are fixed.
</TipBox>
