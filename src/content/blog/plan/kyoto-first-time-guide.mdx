Kyoto rewards early mornings, smart routing and good manners. This page is the whole plan in one place, with links to the detail.

## The Short Version

- **Stay 2–3 days.** One day covers the essentials if you route tightly.
- **Build your days around three areas:** Fushimi Inari, Higashiyama with Gion, and Arashiyama.
- **Start early.** Crowds thin at dawn and after 7pm in every season.
- **Tap an IC card, then walk.** Trains and buses between areas, your feet inside them.
- **Keep your voice down** in the old districts and ask before you photograph people.

## When to Visit

| Season | What you get | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (late Mar–Apr) | Cherry blossoms, best from the last week of March to the first week of April | Dense crowds. Book 3–6 months ahead |
| Late May–early June | Warm days, green gardens, far fewer people | Rainy season usually starts in early June |
| Autumn (Nov–early Dec) | Maple colour, peaking from late November to early December | Prices spike and rooms fill fast |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | The quietest and cheapest months, sometimes snow on temple roofs | Cold. Bring layers |

For value, aim for late May or early November before the leaves peak. In sakura season the busiest spots are [Maruyama Park](https://maps.google.com/?q=Maruyama+Park+Kyoto), the [Philosopher's Path](https://maps.google.com/?q=Philosopher%27s+Path+Kyoto) and the Kamo River.

## Where to Stay

- **Gion / Higashiyama:** the "old Kyoto" choice. Walk to [Kiyomizu-dera](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kiyomizudera+Kyoto) and [Yasaka Shrine](https://maps.google.com/?q=Yasaka+Shrine+Kyoto) without a bus. Daytime crowds and slightly higher prices.
- **Downtown (Kawaramachi / Karasuma):** the practical choice. Near [Nishiki Market](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nishiki+Market+Kyoto), the subway and the Keihan line, with restaurants at every price.
- **Kyoto Station area:** best for day trips to Nara, Osaka or Hiroshima. Budget and business hotels beside the main transit hub.

## Getting Around

  src="/images/blog/inline/kyoto-station-arrival.jpg"

- **IC card:** ICOCA, Suica or PASMO. One card covers JR, subway, Keihan, Hankyu and city buses.
- **Trains:** faster and more predictable than buses. JR goes to Arashiyama and Inari, Keihan to Fushimi Inari and the south.
- **Buses:** flat ¥230. They reach the temples trains miss but crawl from 9am to 5pm on weekends. Bus 205 goes to Kinkakuji, 206 to Higashiyama and Gion.
- **Day pass:** the old ¥700 bus pass no longer exists. The ¥1,100 Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass only pays off on a bus-heavy day.
- **Taxis:** clean, safe and metered. Short rides in the city usually cost ¥800–¥2,000, and most take credit cards.
- **Walking:** do Higashiyama on foot, from Kiyomizu-dera down [Sannenzaka](https://maps.google.com/?q=Sannenzaka+Kyoto) and [Ninenzaka](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ninenzaka+Kyoto) to [Gion](https://maps.google.com/?q=Gion+Kyoto).

More: [How to Get Around Kyoto](/blog/plan/transport-kyoto)

## What to See in 1, 2 or 3 Days

- **Day 1:** [Fushimi Inari](https://maps.google.com/?q=Fushimi+Inari+Taisha+Kyoto) at dawn (arrive by 6am), then Kiyomizu-dera and the Higashiyama walk, an optional afternoon at [Kinkaku-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kinkaku-ji+Kyoto), and Gion in the evening.
- **Day 2:** Arashiyama at sunrise (bamboo grove by 7am), the Tenryu-ji garden next door, the Philosopher's Path in the afternoon, dinner in [Pontocho](https://maps.google.com/?q=Pontocho+Kyoto).
- **Day 3:** the northern loop of Kinkaku-ji, [Ryoan-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ryoan-ji+Kyoto) and Ninnaji, or a day trip to [Nara](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nara+Japan), 45 minutes by express train.

Three things that save time:

- At Fushimi Inari the most photogenic gates are in the first 30–45 minutes. You do not need to reach the summit.
- The bamboo grove is only about 500 meters long. Pair it with Tenryu-ji.
- [Nijo Castle](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nijo+Castle+Kyoto) is the sight first-timers skip and should not. Allow 90 minutes.

Four to six major sights over two days is comfortable. More than that and the temples blur together.

## Manners

<TipBox type="warning" title="TEMPLE & SHRINE MANNERS">
Take your shoes off where a sign or entry mat asks. Rinse your hands at the fountain before you approach a shrine hall. Do not eat while walking on narrow temple streets. Keep your voice low in residential lanes, above all in Gion after dark. A red "no camera" sign means no photos, and always ask before photographing anyone in kimono.
</TipBox>

- **Praying at a shrine:** bow twice, clap twice, bow once.
- **Tipping:** not expected. A sincere "arigatou gozaimasu" is enough.
- **Safety:** Kyoto is extremely safe at night.

## Budget (Mid-Range)

| Category | Estimated cost |
|---|---|
| Temple / shrine entries | ¥400–¥1,000 each |
| Lunch | ¥1,000–¥1,800 |
| Dinner | ¥2,000–¥4,000 |
| Daily transit | ¥900–¥1,200 |
| Snacks / coffee | ¥500–¥800 |
| **Daily total with a room** | **¥12,000–¥18,000** |

Cards work at mid-range restaurants, hotels and convenience stores. Small shrines, vending machines, coin lockers and old shops want cash, and 7-Eleven and Japan Post ATMs take foreign cards.
