Kyoto's subway has only two lines, and the buses that reach the famous temples are the ones most likely to sit in traffic. Pick the right option for each trip and you save hours.

## The Short Version

- **Get an IC card** (ICOCA or Suica). One tap pays for every bus, subway and train.
- **Trains for distance, buses for the temples trains miss, your feet inside a neighborhood.**
- **Skip the day pass** unless you ride five or more times in one day.
- **Stay off buses 8:00–9:30am and 5:00–7:00pm**, and all day in cherry blossom and autumn peak weeks.
- **Taxis pay off for 2–4 people.** A ride across town is about ¥2,000.

## Which Option for Which Trip

| Journey | Best option | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Kyoto Station → [Fushimi Inari](https://maps.google.com/?q=Fushimi+Inari+Taisha+Kyoto) | JR Nara Line to Inari. The gate is across the street | 5 min |
| Kyoto Station → [Arashiyama](https://maps.google.com/?q=Arashiyama+Kyoto) | JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama | 15 min |
| Kyoto Station → [Kinkakuji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kinkaku-ji+Kyoto) | Bus 205 from stand B3 to Kinkakuji-michi, or a taxi | 40 min |
| Kyoto Station → [Kiyomizudera](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kiyomizudera+Kyoto) | Bus 206 from stand D2 to Gojozaka, then 10 min uphill on foot | 20–30 min |
| Gion → Arashiyama | Walk to Hankyu Kyoto-Kawaramachi, Hankyu to Arashiyama (change at Katsura) | 35–40 min |
| [Nijo Castle](https://maps.google.com/?q=Nijo+Castle+Kyoto) → Gion | Tozai subway line to Higashiyama, then 10 min on foot | 20 min |
| Fushimi Inari → Gion | Keihan Line from Fushimi-Inari to Gion-Shijo | 10 min |

Two traps: a *Rapid* 205 skips Kinkakuji, and for the shrine you want Keihan **Fushimi-Inari** station, not Fushimi-Momoyama.

## IC Cards

- **Which one:** ICOCA (sold at [Kyoto Station](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kyoto+Station+Japan)), Suica and PASMO all work the same. If you have one from Tokyo, bring it.
- **On your phone:** add Suica to Apple Wallet or Google Pay before you fly. You top up by credit card and skip the ticket-machine queue.
- **Top up:** JR ticket machines or any convenience store. Start with ¥2,000–¥3,000.
- **How to tap:** on trains, tap in and tap out. On city buses, board at the rear door and tap at the front when you get off.

## Buses

City buses reach nearly every temple for a flat ¥230, but they run on surface roads and fill up fast.

- **Bus 206:** Kyoto Station to Kiyomizudera, [Gion](https://maps.google.com/?q=Gion+Kyoto) and Higashiyama. The busiest tourist bus in the city.
- **Bus 205:** Kyoto Station to Kinkakuji.
- **Bus 202 / 207:** alternatives into Higashiyama when 206 is full.
- **Paying:** IC card or exact change. Drivers give no change, but there is a change machine at the front.
- **Finding your stop:** Google Maps shows live departures and which stand to board from.

<TipBox type="warning" title="OLD ADVICE TO IGNORE">
<strong>The ¥700 bus day pass is gone.</strong> It stopped being accepted in March 2024. The replacement is the Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass at ¥1,100 (¥550 for children), which only pays off from about five rides a day.<br /><br />
<strong>Raku Bus routes 100, 101 and 102 no longer run.</strong> Use 205, 206 and the subway instead.<br /><br />
<strong>Fares are set to change in fiscal 2027.</strong> Kyoto has announced a plan to charge visitors roughly ¥350–400 per ride and residents about ¥200. Check before a 2027 trip.
</TipBox>

## Trains

Faster than a bus for almost any cross-city trip.

- **Subway:** two lines. Karasuma runs north–south, Tozai runs east–west (Nijo Castle, Higashiyama).
- **JR:** the Nara Line for Fushimi Inari, the Sagano Line for Arashiyama.
- **Keihan:** along the Kamo River from Sanjo and Gion-Shijo south to Fushimi, then on to Osaka.
- **Hankyu:** from Kyoto-Kawaramachi to Arashiyama, changing at Katsura. Older maps call the station just "Kawaramachi".

## Taxis

- **Cost:** about ¥500 for the first kilometre. Gion to Kinkakuji runs about ¥2,000, so four people pay ¥500 each and save 40 minutes on the bus.
- **Late night:** about 20% extra between 10pm and 5am.
- **Doors:** the rear door opens and closes by itself. Leave the handle alone.
- **Apps:** GO and DiDi both work in English and show a price before you confirm.
- **Paying:** most take IC cards and credit cards. Ask before you get in.

## Walking and Cycling

Higashiyama and Gion are best on foot. From Kiyomizudera down [Sannenzaka](https://maps.google.com/?q=Sannenzaka+Kyoto) and [Ninenzaka](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ninenzaka+Kyoto) into Gion is about 3 km, mostly flat, and no bus goes where the good lanes are.

Central Kyoto is flat and easy by bike. Rentals cost ¥1,000–¥1,500 a day, and an e-bike for the eastern hills is ¥500–¥1,000 more. Park only in marked bicycle lots, or the bike gets impounded and costs ¥2,000 or more to recover. The Kamo River path is the best ride in the city: flat and free of traffic.

## Day Trips

The same IC card works across Kansai. [Osaka](/blog/day-trips/osaka-from-kyoto) is about 30 minutes by regular train, [Nara](/blog/day-trips/nara-from-kyoto) 35–45 minutes, and [Uji](/blog/day-trips/uji-from-kyoto) 17 minutes on the JR Nara Line.
