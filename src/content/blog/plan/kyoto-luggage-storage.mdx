Kyoto is a city of stone steps, narrow lanes, gravel temple paths, and crowded buses — all of which are considerably worse with a suitcase. The arrival day and the departure day are where most trips lose hours, and both are solvable. This guide covers what lockers cost, what to do when they are all taken, and how the luggage-forwarding network that locals use actually works.

## The Rule That Solves Most of It

**Do not carry luggage around Kyoto.** Either send it ahead to your next hotel, or leave it somewhere and collect it later. Almost every "we wasted the morning" story starts with someone deciding to take a suitcase on Bus 206.

## Coin Lockers

Sizes and prices are broadly standard across the city: around **¥400 small**, **¥500 medium**, **¥700 large**. Most modern lockers take IC cards as well as cash, which saves hunting for coins.

Two details that catch people out:

**Charging is by calendar day, not by 24 hours.** Lockers roll over at midnight (2am at some Kyoto Station banks), so a bag left from 6pm to 10am the next morning costs two days, not one.

**Most have a three-day limit.** After that, contents are removed and held by station staff, and retrieving them is a bureaucratic afternoon you do not want.

<TipBox type="warning" title="KYOTO STATION LOCKERS FILL EARLY">
Kyoto Station has over 1,500 lockers on the Hachijo side alone, and in cherry blossom and autumn seasons they are still effectively full by mid-morning. Large lockers go first. If your plan depends on finding one at 11am on a November Saturday, have a backup.
</TipBox>

<TipBox type="local" title="STORE AT YOUR DESTINATION, NOT AT KYOTO STATION">
Everyone tries Kyoto Station, which is why it is full. The lockers at Gion-Shijo, Kyoto-Kawaramachi, Saga-Arashiyama, and Inari stations are far less contested, and storing your bag at the station nearest your first sight saves you a return trip across the city to collect it.
</TipBox>

## Staffed Storage

When lockers fail you, staffed counters do not.

**Crosta Kyoto** on the B1 floor of Kyoto Station is the main one: it accepts bags too large for any locker, and it also runs same-day delivery to Kyoto hotels — hand over your suitcase in the morning and find it waiting at your accommodation.

Several private storage counters operate near Kyoto Station and in the downtown Kawaramachi area, and many hotels will hold your bags both before check-in and after checkout at no charge. That last option is free, obvious, and routinely forgotten: if you are staying in Kyoto that night anyway, drop your bags at the hotel first thing, even if your room is not ready.

## Takkyubin: Sending Bags Ahead

This is the part visitors most often do not know exists, and it changes how a multi-city trip feels.

Japan's *takkyubin* courier services move luggage between addresses cheaply and with total reliability. Hand a suitcase to your hotel front desk in Tokyo before the afternoon cut-off, fill in a short form, pay roughly **¥2,000–2,500**, and it is at your Kyoto hotel the next day. You travel with a day bag.

**How to do it:** ask the front desk for *takkyubin* (or "luggage forwarding"). They complete the form with you — you need the destination hotel's name, address, phone number, and your check-in date. Hotels handle this constantly and staff will walk you through it even with no shared language.

**What it costs you:** one night without your main bag. Pack a change of clothes, chargers, and anything you need for the evening into your day bag.

<TipBox type="budget" title="THE SHINKANSEN LUGGAGE MATH">
If your suitcase's three dimensions total more than 160 cm, the Shinkansen requires you to reserve a specific oversized-baggage seat in advance or pay a fee on board. Forwarding the bag for ¥2,000–2,500 sidesteps that rule entirely, and you make the journey unencumbered. See [Tokyo to Kyoto by Shinkansen](/blog/plan/tokyo-to-kyoto-shinkansen) for the rule in detail.
</TipBox>

<TipBox type="warning" title="AIRPORT DELIVERY NEEDS TWO DAYS">
Sending luggage from a Kyoto hotel to Kansai or Haneda airport for collection before your flight works well — but allow two days, not one. Next-day airport delivery is not reliable on every route, and a bag that misses your flight is a genuinely serious problem. Confirm the arrival date with the front desk when you hand it over.
</TipBox>

## Arrival and Departure Days

**Arrival day.** Send your bag ahead from your previous hotel if you can. If not, drop it at your Kyoto hotel before check-in — they will hold it — and start sightseeing immediately. Do not begin your first day by finding a locker.

**Departure day.** Check out, leave your bags with the hotel, and use the last few hours properly. Collect them on the way to the station. If you are catching an early Shinkansen, a locker at Kyoto Station booked the previous evening removes all morning stress.

**Day trips.** Take nothing. [Nara](/blog/day-trips/nara-from-kyoto), [Osaka](/blog/day-trips/osaka-from-kyoto), and [Uji](/blog/day-trips/uji-from-kyoto) are all half-day trips from a Kyoto base — your luggage should stay in your hotel room.
