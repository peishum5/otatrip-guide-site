Kyoto is stone steps, narrow lanes, gravel temple paths and crowded buses, and all of them are worse with a suitcase. Arrival day and departure day are where most trips lose hours, and both are easy to fix.

## The Short Version

- **Do not carry luggage around Kyoto.** Send it ahead or leave it somewhere and collect it later.
- **Your hotel holds bags for free** before check-in and after checkout. Use that first.
- **Kyoto Station lockers are full by mid-morning in peak season.** Use a locker at the station nearest your first sight, or a staffed counter.
- **Send bags hotel to hotel by *takkyubin*** for roughly ¥2,000–3,500, overnight.
- **Sending a bag to the airport takes two days**, not one.

## Coin Lockers

- **Prices:** around ¥400 small, ¥500 medium and ¥700 large across the city. Most take IC cards as well as cash.
- **You pay by calendar day, not by 24 hours.** Lockers roll over at midnight (2am at some Kyoto Station banks), so a bag left from 6pm to 10am the next morning costs two days.
- **Most have a three-day limit.** After that, station staff remove the contents, and getting them back takes an afternoon of paperwork.
- **Where to look:** Gion-Shijo, Kyoto-Kawaramachi, Saga-Arashiyama and Inari stations are far less contested than Kyoto Station. Storing near your first sight also saves a trip back across the city.

<TipBox type="warning" title="KYOTO STATION LOCKERS FILL EARLY">
Kyoto Station has well over a thousand lockers, most of them on the Hachijo side, and in cherry blossom and autumn seasons they are still full by mid-morning. Large lockers go first. Have a backup.
</TipBox>

## Staffed Storage

- **Crosta Kyoto (B1 floor, Kyoto Station):** takes bags too large for any locker, and delivers to listed Kyoto hotels the same day (¥1,500 a bag, drop off by 2pm). Storage is ¥1,000 a day.
- **Private counters:** near Kyoto Station and in the downtown Kawaramachi area.
- **Your hotel:** many hold bags free before check-in and after checkout. Drop them first thing, even if your room is not ready.

## Takkyubin: Sending Bags Ahead

- **How:** ask the front desk for *takkyubin* or "luggage forwarding" before the afternoon cut-off. You need the next hotel's name, address and phone number, and your check-in date. Staff fill in the form with you.
- **Cost:** roughly ¥2,000–3,500, depending on size and distance. The bag arrives the next day.
- **The catch:** one night without your main bag. Keep a change of clothes and your chargers with you.
- **Shinkansen:** a suitcase whose three dimensions total more than 160 cm needs a reserved oversized-baggage seat, or you pay a fee on board. Forwarding avoids the rule. See [Tokyo to Kyoto by Shinkansen](/blog/plan/tokyo-to-kyoto-shinkansen).
- **To the airport:** allow two days, and confirm the arrival date with the front desk.

## Arrival, Departure and Day Trips

- **Arrival day:** send the bag ahead from your previous hotel, or drop it at your Kyoto hotel before check-in.
- **Departure day:** check out, leave the bags at the hotel, and collect them on the way to the station.
- **Day trips:** take nothing. [Nara](/blog/day-trips/nara-from-kyoto), [Osaka](/blog/day-trips/osaka-from-kyoto) and [Uji](/blog/day-trips/uji-from-kyoto) are half-day trips, so the luggage stays in your room.
