The Tokaido Shinkansen runs from Tokyo to Kyoto in a little over two hours, several times an hour. Three choices matter: which train, whether to reserve, and whether your suitcase needs its own reservation.

## The Short Version

- **Paying by ticket? Take the Nozomi.** It is the fastest and most frequent train.
- **Have a Japan Rail Pass? Take the Hikari.** It is fully covered and only 25 minutes slower.
- **Measure your suitcase.** If its three dimensions total 160 cm or more, you must reserve an oversized-baggage seat.
- **Book 21 days ahead on Smart EX** to save about ¥2,000.
- **Sit on the right (seats D and E)** for Mount Fuji.

## Nozomi or Hikari?

| | Nozomi | Hikari |
|---|---|---|
| Journey time | 2 hr 15 min | 2 hr 40 min |
| Frequency | Several per hour | Roughly 2 per hour |
| Reserved fare | ~¥14,170 | ~¥13,850 |
| Non-reserved fare | ~¥13,320 | ~¥13,320 |
| Japan Rail Pass | Supplement ~¥4,960 | Fully covered |

With a Japan Rail Pass, the Nozomi supplement buys you only 25 minutes. **Smart EX**, the online booking service for this line, takes foreign credit cards.

## Reserved or Non-Reserved?

- **Non-reserved:** the front few cars. Queue at the platform marking and take any free seat. On a normal weekday you will get one.
- **Reserve when:** you are a group and want to sit together, you have a large suitcase, or you travel in a peak week.

<TipBox type="warning" title="PEAK TRAVEL WEEKS ARE DIFFERENT">
In Golden Week (late April to early May), Obon (mid-August) and the New Year period, reserve well ahead. Nozomi trains run with reserved seats only in those weeks, and the non-reserved cars on other trains fill completely, with people standing for the whole trip.
</TipBox>

## The Oversized Luggage Rule

Add up your suitcase's three dimensions.

- **Under 160 cm:** nothing to do. It goes on the overhead rack or in front of your knees. Most standard check-in suitcases fall under this.
- **160 to 250 cm:** book a "seat with oversized baggage area", a last-row seat with the space behind it. The reservation is free. Boarding without one means a fee on the train.
- **Over 250 cm:** not allowed on board.

**The easy way out:** send the suitcase from your Tokyo hotel to your Kyoto hotel by *takkyubin* courier for roughly ¥2,000–2,500, overnight. See [Luggage in Kyoto](/blog/plan/kyoto-luggage-storage).

## On the Day

- **Tokyo Station:** platforms 14–19, behind their own gates. Allow 15 minutes if you transfer from a regular JR line.
- **Food:** buy an *ekiben* (station bento) before you board. Trolley service has ended on most trains.
- **On board:** power outlets and free Wi-Fi.
- **Mount Fuji:** on the right-hand side, about 40–45 minutes after Tokyo. November to February gives the best odds. Summer haze often hides it.
- **Arriving:** the Shinkansen platforms are on the south side of Kyoto Station. The subway, buses and taxis are on the Karasuma (north) side. See the [Kyoto Station Guide](/blog/plan/kyoto-station-guide).
