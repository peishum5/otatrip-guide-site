There is no single best month for Kyoto. The famous weeks are the most beautiful and the most crowded, and the quiet months are better than their reputation.

## The Short Version

- **Best scenery:** late March to early April for cherry blossoms, late November to early December for autumn leaves. Also the busiest and most expensive weeks.
- **Best all-round:** late May. Warm, green and quiet.
- **Cheapest and quietest:** January, February, and December after the 20th. June is also cheaper.
- **Avoid if you can:** Golden Week, around April 29 to May 5.
- **In every month:** the famous places are crowded from about 10am to 5pm and quiet outside those hours.

## Month by Month

| Month | Weather | Crowds | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jan** | 2–8°C, clear and dry | Quiet after Jan 3 | Lowest hotel rates of the year |
| **Feb** | Cold | Quiet | Setsubun on Feb 3. Plum blossoms from late in the month |
| **Mar** | 8°C early, 15–18°C late | Building fast | First cherry blossoms in late March. Prices jump from mid-month |
| **Apr** | Mild, some rain | Packed | Peak bloom around the first week, then Golden Week |
| **May** | 20–25°C | Quiet after May 6 | Fresh green maples and the Aoi Matsuri |
| **Jun** | Humid, rainy season from early in the month | Quiet | Hydrangeas, lower hotel rates |
| **Jul** | 33–36°C, humid | Medium | Gion Matsuri all month |
| **Aug** | 35°C and up | Medium to busy | Gozan Okuribi bonfires on Aug 16 |
| **Sep** | 22–25°C by late month | Quiet | Typhoon season, but most days are fine. Cheaper |
| **Oct** | 15–22°C, clear | Quiet, building late | First maple colour at the end of the month |
| **Nov** | Cool and crisp | Packed from mid-month | Peak autumn leaves late in the month. Prices at their yearly high, with April |
| **Dec** | Cold and dry | Busy in the first week, very quiet after the 20th | Last autumn leaves early in the month, illuminations, clear mountain views |

<TipBox type="warning" title="BOOK EARLY OR MOVE YOUR DATES">
<strong>Cherry blossoms and autumn leaves:</strong> book rooms three to six months ahead. For late November, book by August. The best ryokan near Higashiyama are gone by September.<br /><br />
<strong>Golden Week:</strong> trains, buses and sights are extremely crowded. Finish your Kyoto stay before April 27 or arrive from about May 7. If you cannot, book rooms and Shinkansen seats two to three months ahead.<br /><br />
<strong>July and August:</strong> 35°C with high humidity is normal. Carry water, start early and take an air-conditioned break every hour or two.
</TipBox>

## Dates Worth Planning Around

**Cherry blossoms (late March to mid-April)**

- [Maruyama Park](https://maps.google.com/?q=Maruyama+Park+Kyoto) and the [Philosopher's Path](https://maps.google.com/?q=Philosopher%27s+Path+Kyoto) are the famous spots and the most crowded. For quieter ones, see the [10 best sakura spots in Kyoto](/blog/seasonal/kyoto-cherry-blossom-spots).
- Timing shifts by a week or more each year. Check the bloom forecast before you lock in dates.
- The falling petals in mid-April are, for many people, better than full bloom.

**Autumn leaves (mid-November to early December)**

- [Tofuku-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Tofukuji+Kyoto), [Eikan-do](https://maps.google.com/?q=Eikan-do+Kyoto) and [Kiyomizu-dera](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kiyomizudera+Kyoto) are the most popular. Evening illuminations extend the viewing hours.
- At Tofuku-ji, arrive before 8am to beat the queue for the Tsutenkyo Bridge.

**Festivals and events**

- **Setsubun, Feb 3:** bean-throwing at [Yoshida Shrine](https://maps.google.com/?q=Yoshida+Shrine+Kyoto) and [Heian Jingu](https://maps.google.com/?q=Heian+Shrine+Kyoto). Crowded, festive and free.
- **Plum blossoms, peaking around Feb 25:** [Kitano Tenmangu](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kitano+Tenmangu+Kyoto). As pretty as sakura with a fraction of the crowds.
- **Aoi Matsuri, May 15:** an imperial procession from the [Kyoto Imperial Palace](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kyoto+Imperial+Palace) to [Shimogamo Shrine](https://maps.google.com/?q=Shimogamo+Shrine+Kyoto) and [Kamigamo Shrine](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kamigamo+Shrine+Kyoto). Free from the roadside.
- **Hydrangeas, June:** [Mimurotoji Temple](https://maps.google.com/?q=Mimurotoji+Temple+Uji) near Uji.
- **Gion Matsuri, July:** the lit floats on the evenings of July 14–16, with street stalls on the 15th and 16th, then the float procession on July 17. See the [Gion Matsuri guide](/blog/seasonal/gion-matsuri).
- **Gozan Okuribi, Aug 16:** bonfires in the shape of characters on five mountains around the city, lit from 8pm.
- **Jidai Matsuri, Oct 22:** a historical costume parade leaving from the Imperial Palace. Free to watch.
- **Toji flea market, the 21st of every month:** one of Japan's largest antique markets, in the grounds of [Toji Temple](https://maps.google.com/?q=Toji+Temple+Kyoto).

## What to Pack

| Season | Bring |
|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Layers (8°C mornings, 20°C afternoons), a light rain jacket or compact umbrella |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | Breathable fabrics, sun cover, a cooling towel or hand fan, electrolyte drinks, an umbrella that doubles as a parasol |
| Autumn (Sep–Nov) | Layers, a light to medium jacket by November, a compact umbrella for typhoon rain |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Warm coat, scarf, hat and gloves, a thermal layer, waterproof shoes in case of snow. [Kinkaku-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kinkaku-ji+Kyoto) in snow is worth the cold |

In every season, wear walking shoes you have already broken in. Expect 15,000–20,000 steps a day on stone paths and temple stairs.

## Where to Stay by Season

- **Spring and autumn peaks:** pay the premium to sleep within walking distance of the sights, in Gion or Higashiyama. Buses are slow and full.
- **Summer and winter:** the Kyoto Station area is practical and often better value, with air-conditioned shopping and easy JR access.
