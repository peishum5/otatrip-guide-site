Kyoto Station is the second-largest station building in Japan, serves five separate rail operators, and hides its bus terminal behind a fifteen-storey atrium. Almost every Kyoto trip passes through it several times, and almost every visitor loses twenty minutes to it at least once. This guide covers the layout that matters: which exit, which stand, and how long each transfer really takes.

## The One Thing to Understand: North vs South

Kyoto Station has two sides, and they are not equivalent.

**Karasuma (north / central) exit** faces the city. The bus terminal, the subway entrance, the taxi rank, the Kyoto Tower, and the walk into downtown are all here. This is the correct exit roughly 90% of the time.

**Hachijo (south) exit** faces away from the city. It serves the Kintetsu line to Nara, the airport limousine bus, a cluster of business hotels, and most of the coin lockers. There is little else.

Crossing between them means walking through or around the station building — 10 to 15 minutes with luggage. Getting this wrong is the single most common Kyoto Station mistake.

<TipBox type="warning" title="THE HACHIJO TRAP">
If you arrive on the airport limousine bus, you land at Hachijo — the wrong side for the subway, the city buses, and downtown. Budget the extra 10–15 minutes to cross, or take a taxi from the Hachijo rank rather than dragging suitcases through the concourse.
</TipBox>

## The Bus Terminal

The bus terminal sits directly outside the Karasuma exit, arranged as numbered stands (A1–A3, B1–B3, C, D1–D3) around a large plaza. Each stand has a digital board listing the next departures.

| Destination | Bus | Stand | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kinkakuji (Golden Pavilion) | 205 | B3 | ~40 min |
| Kiyomizudera (Gojo-zaka) | 206 | D2 | ~15 min |
| Gion / Yasaka Shrine | 206 | D2 | ~20 min |
| Ginkakuji (Silver Pavilion) | 5 | A1 | ~40 min |

Two things worth knowing. First, **do not board a "Rapid" 205** if you are going to Kinkakuji — the rapid service skips it. Second, the buses that matter to tourists are also the ones that fill at the terminus, so if you can board at the very start of the route you will get a seat; if you join further along in peak season you probably will not.

<TipBox type="local" title="THE SUBWAY SHORTCUT TO KINKAKUJI">
Riding Bus 205 all the way from Kyoto Station to Kinkakuji takes about 40 minutes and is frequently standing-room-only. Instead, take the Karasuma subway line north to Kitaoji (about 13 minutes, guaranteed seat), then Bus 204 or 205 for the last 11 minutes. Same destination, less of your day spent in traffic.
</TipBox>

## Trains: Who Runs What

Five operators use Kyoto Station, on different levels and behind different gates:

- **JR local and regional lines** (Nara Line for Fushimi Inari, Sagano Line for Arashiyama, Kyoto Line for Osaka) — main ground-floor gates.
- **Tokaido Shinkansen** — its own gates on the south side. Separate ticket, separate barrier.
- **Haruka airport express** — platform 30, at the far south-west end. A long walk from the central concourse.
- **Kintetsu line** (for Nara) — a separate station on the Hachijo side, one level down.
- **Karasuma subway line** — underground, accessed from the Karasuma side.

**Transfer times to allow:** 15 minutes between regular JR and the Shinkansen, 10 minutes to reach the Haruka platform, 10 minutes to the Kintetsu or subway. These are walking times inside a very large building, not queue times.

## Getting to the Main Sights

| From Kyoto Station to | Best option | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Fushimi Inari | JR Nara Line to Inari (2 stops) | 5 min |
| Arashiyama | JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama | 15 min |
| Gion | Bus 206 from stand D2 | ~20 min |
| Kiyomizudera | Bus 206 to Gojo-zaka, then 10 min uphill | ~25 min |
| Kinkakuji | Subway to Kitaoji, then Bus 204/205 | ~25 min |
| Downtown (Shijo) | Karasuma subway, 2 stops | 5 min |
| Nara | Kintetsu Express from Hachijo side | 45–50 min |
| Osaka | JR Special Rapid | 30 min |

## Luggage, Food, and Waiting

**Coin lockers** run about ¥400 (small), ¥500 (medium), and ¥700 (large), and most take IC cards as well as coins. There are over 1,500 on the Hachijo side alone, plus banks near the Karasuma exit and in the Porta underground mall. In peak season they are largely full by 10am — if you are relying on one, arrive early or go straight to a staffed service.

**Crosta Kyoto**, on the B1 floor, is the staffed alternative: it takes bags too large for a locker and also runs same-day delivery to Kyoto hotels. If you are arriving before check-in or leaving after checkout, this is usually the better answer than hunting for an empty locker.

**Eating.** The station building has a full restaurant floor (Kyoto Ramen Koji on the 10th floor gathers regional ramen shops), Isetan department store's food hall in the basement, and the Porta underground mall. It is a genuinely good place to eat, which is useful on a rainy arrival day.

<TipBox type="tip" title="THE FREE VIEWPOINT">
The Skyway walkway and rooftop terrace on the upper floors of the station building are free and open to the public, with a wide view north over the city to the mountains. It is a decent way to spend the twenty minutes before a train, and considerably cheaper than the Kyoto Tower observation deck across the road.
</TipBox>
