Gion Matsuri fills the whole of July, but two processions and the evenings before them are what most visitors come for. Choose your dates first, because the crowds differ enormously from one day to the next.

## The Short Version

- **The main procession is July 17 at 9am.** 23 floats, about three hours, free from the roadside.
- **The best evenings are Yoiyama, July 14–16.** The floats are lit and the streets fill with people in yukata.
- **Want fewer people? Come July 21–24.** The second Yoiyama and procession are much calmer.
- **Want almost no one? Come July 10–13** and watch the floats being built in the streets.
- **Book your hotel 4–6 months ahead**, and take the heat seriously.

## The July Calendar

| Date | What happens | Crowds |
|---|---|---|
| July 1 | Opening ceremony at Yasaka Shrine | Quiet |
| July 10–13 | Floats are assembled in the streets around Shijo-Karasuma. Walk right up to them | Quiet |
| July 14–16 | **Yoiyama**, the eve of the first procession. Floats lit by lanterns, festival music after dark | Extreme. Over 100,000 people a night |
| July 17 | **First procession** (Mae-Matsuri), 9am, 23 floats | Extreme. Over 200,000 spectators |
| July 21–23 | **Second Yoiyama**. Lit floats and music, without the crush | Busy but easy to walk |
| July 24 | **Second procession** (Ato-Matsuri), 11 floats | Much smaller. Best for photos |
| July 31 | Closing ceremony at Yasaka Shrine | Quiet |

The festival began in 869 as a ritual at Yasaka Shrine to end a plague. The floats, called *yamaboko*, stand about 25 meters tall and weigh up to 12 tons, and are hung with centuries-old tapestries from Europe, the Middle East and China.

## Yoiyama Evenings

- **Arrive by 6pm.** The peak is 8pm–10pm, and the floats look best after dark.
- **July 15 and 16 are the big nights.** The streets around Shijo-Karasuma close to cars in the evening and fill with food stalls.
- **July 21–23 is a different mood.** The floats are lit and the music plays, but there are no food stalls and the streets stay open to cars. It is the calmer choice.
- **Food stalls:** takoyaki, yakitori, shaved ice and okonomiyaki at ¥400–¥800 each.
- **Yukata:** welcome, and many locals wear one. Rental shops across the city offer them in July.

## Watching the Processions

- **Route on July 17:** from Shijo-Karasuma east along Shijo Street, north up Kawaramachi Street, then west along Oike Street.
- **Where to stand:** anywhere along the route is free. Roadside spots on Oike Street fill by 7am. For the front row, arrive at least two hours early.
- **What to watch for:** the corner turns, called *tsuji-mawashi*. Teams lay bamboo under the wheels and haul the float around 90 degrees by rope.
- **Reserved seats:** ¥6,000–¥8,000 for general seats in 2026. Sales opened in early June, and they sell out fast.
- **July 24:** fewer floats and a far smaller crowd. This procession was only restored in 2014.

<TipBox type="warning" title="JULY HEAT IS SERIOUS">
July in Kyoto is hot and very humid, with days regularly reaching 34°C. Carry water and a small towel, stay out of direct sun while you wait for the procession, and use convenience stores as air-conditioned breaks. Check the time of your last train before you go out for the evening.
</TipBox>
