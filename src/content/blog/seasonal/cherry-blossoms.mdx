Cherry blossom timing in Kyoto moves by a week or more each year, so booking a sakura trip is partly a gamble. Learn how the forecast works and keep a plan for arriving early or late.

## The Short Version

- **Peak is usually late March to early April.** Over the past decade it has landed anywhere from March 20 to April 10.
- **Full bloom is short.** The best days number five to seven, and forecasts firm up about two weeks before.
- **Book your hotel 3–6 months ahead.** Kyoto sells out in peak week.
- **Go before 9am or after dark.** Noon to 6pm is the worst of the crowds.
- **Missed the peak? Go to Ninna-ji or Hirano Shrine.** Both bloom later than the rest of the city.

## How the Bloom Works

| Stage | What you see |
|---|---|
| First bloom (*kaika*) | Five or six flowers open on a benchmark tree. The official start |
| Full bloom (*mankai*) | 80% or more of the flowers open. The best 5–7 days |
| Falling petals (*hanafubuki*) | Pink flurries in the wind. Still beautiful |

- **Where to check:** the Japan Meteorological Corporation, Weathernews and Tenki.jp all publish sakura forecasts. Start looking in February.
- **When to trust it:** about two weeks before bloom. Fix your exact days then.
- **Rain:** light rain is fine. Heavy rain strips the petals and can end the peak in a day.

## If You Arrive Early or Late

- **Late:** Ninna-ji has *omuro zakura*, a short variety that blooms 1–2 weeks after most others and carries the season into mid-April. ¥500 during bloom.
- **Almost any time:** Hirano Shrine has about 60 varieties that bloom in turn over 4–5 weeks, from late March through late April. Free outside its festival, and few foreign visitors come.
- **Just after peak:** the falling petals are worth seeing. Do not write the trip off.

## Where to Go

| Spot | Cost | Best time |
|---|---|---|
| Maruyama Park | Free | Before 9am, or after 8pm for the lit weeping cherry |
| Philosopher's Path | Free | 7–9am. A 2 km canal walk under a full canopy |
| Kiyomizudera | ¥500 | Early. The paths on the back slope behind the temple are far quieter. See the [Kiyomizudera guide](/blog/sights/kiyomizudera) |
| Fushimi Inari | Free | Before 8am. Blossoms against the torii on the lower slopes. See the [Fushimi Inari guide](/blog/sights/fushimi-inari) |

<TipBox type="warning" title="NIGHT ILLUMINATION QUEUES">
Kiyomizudera and Kodaiji have very long queues in peak week. For Kiyomizudera, arrive at least 30 minutes before the 6pm opening. Nijo Castle costs more and is often the easiest night event to get into without a long wait.
</TipBox>
