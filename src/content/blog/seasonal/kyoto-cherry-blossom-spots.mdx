Kyoto has more good sakura spots than you can see in one trip. Pick two or three areas that match what you want, and go early.

## The Short Version

- **First trip:** Maruyama Park, Philosopher's Path, Kiyomizudera and Keage Incline.
- **Hate crowds:** Kamo River, Okazaki Canal, Daigoji and Ninna-ji.
- **Arriving late:** Ninna-ji first, then Hirano Shrine.
- **Go at 7am–9am.** Come back out after dark for Maruyama Park and Gion.
- **Do not try to collect all ten.** Timing matters more than the length of your list.

## The 10 Spots

| Spot | Why go | When to go |
|---|---|---|
| [Maruyama Park](https://maps.google.com/?q=Maruyama+Park+Kyoto) | The classic hanami park, with a giant weeping cherry lit at night. Right behind Yasaka Shrine | After dark, or before 8am. Packed at midday in peak week. Arrive by 6pm for space in the evening |
| [Philosopher's Path](https://maps.google.com/?q=Philosopher%27s+Path+Kyoto) | A canal walk that becomes a blossom tunnel. Pair it with Nanzenji, Eikando or Ginkaku-ji | By 7am, walking south to north from the Nanzenji end. Packed from 10am to 3pm |
| [Kiyomizudera](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kiyomizudera+Kyoto) | The big view: temple stage, hillside blossoms and the city beyond. See the [Kiyomizudera guide](/blog/sights/kiyomizudera) | At the 6am opening. Crowded, but the scale is worth it |
| [Keage Incline](https://maps.google.com/?q=Keage+Incline+Kyoto) | Old railway tracks under rows of cherry trees. The best photo spot | Before 8am, in morning light. Tour groups arrive from 10am |
| [Okazaki Canal](https://maps.google.com/?q=Okazaki+Canal+Kyoto) | Wide paths and water reflections near Heian Jingu. An easy, comfortable walk | Morning. More space than Maruyama or Kiyomizudera |
| [Kamo River](https://maps.google.com/?q=Kamo+River+Kyoto) | Hanami the local way: walk, sit, snack. No fees and no bookings | Late afternoon, between Sanjo and Shijo bridges. Relaxed even in peak week |
| [Daigoji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Daigoji+Temple+Kyoto) | Large, grand temple grounds that feel less squeezed than the center | Early on a weekday. Busy around the pagoda at weekend midday |
| [Ninna-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ninna-ji+Kyoto) | Late-blooming *omuro zakura*, short trees with blossoms at eye level | Mid-morning, 1–2 weeks after the main bloom. The place to go if you missed the peak |
| [Hirano Shrine](https://maps.google.com/?q=Hirano+Shrine+Kyoto) | Many varieties that bloom one after another, so the window is long | Flexible. Good for repeat visitors and photographers |
| [Arashiyama](https://maps.google.com/?q=Arashiyama+Kyoto) | River, bridge and hills with scattered sakura. A different landscape from eastern Kyoto | Morning. Best as a second-day option |

## One Good Sakura Day

1. Kiyomizudera at opening.
2. Walk down through [Higashiyama](/blog/neighborhoods/higashiyama) to Maruyama Park.
3. Go north to Keage Incline and the Okazaki Canal.
4. Finish on the Philosopher's Path if you still have the energy.
5. Return to Maruyama Park or Gion after dark.

<TipBox type="warning" title="BUSES IN PEAK WEEK">
Bus 206 through Higashiyama runs far over capacity on peak bloom weekends. Walk between spots where you can, and use the Keihan Line and the Tozai subway line for longer hops.<br /><br />
Kiyomizudera's evening illumination runs for about ten days only. Check the dates on the temple's website before you go.
</TipBox>
