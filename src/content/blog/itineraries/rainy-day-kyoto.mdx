Rain is not a lost day in Kyoto. Rock and moss gardens look better wet, the crowds thin out, and there is a full day of things to do under a roof.

## The Short Version

- **In light rain, go outside anyway.** Ryoan-ji, the Arashiyama bamboo grove and the lower gates of Fushimi Inari are better wet and far emptier.
- **In heavy rain, stay under cover.** Nishiki Market, the Teramachi arcade, the Kyoto National Museum and the Manga Museum fill a day.
- **Book something seated.** A tea ceremony or a sake tasting suits a wet afternoon.
- **Wear rubber soles.** Wet stone lanes and temple steps are slippery.
- **Forgot an umbrella?** Any convenience store sells one for ¥500–¥700.

## Better in the Rain

- **Ryoan-ji (¥600):** you view the rock garden from a covered wooden veranda, so the rain is part of the scene. On wet days you may have it almost to yourself.
- **[Arashiyama bamboo grove](/blog/sights/arashiyama-bamboo):** light rain clears the path and leaves mist in the canopy. Come before 9am and wear waterproof shoes.
- **[Fushimi Inari](/blog/sights/fushimi-inari), lower tunnels only:** the gates turn a deeper red and the crowds disappear. Stay on the paved lower loop.
- **Saiho-ji, the moss temple:** a garden carpeted in over 120 kinds of moss, at its best when wet. Reservation only, online or by postcard, several weeks ahead. The ¥3,000+ fee includes a sutra-copying session before the garden walk.

## A Dry Day, Hour by Hour

| Time | Stop | Getting there and cost |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | **[Nishiki Market](/blog/food/nishiki-market).** A covered 400-metre arcade: pickles, tofu, tamagoyaki on a stick. 60–90 min. | 5 min walk from Shijo (subway) or Karasuma (Hankyu) station. Food ¥200–¥800 per item |
| 10:30 | **Teramachi arcade.** A covered shopping street with fans, washi paper, tea and bookshops. 60 min. | 5 min walk from Nishiki Market |
| 12:00 | **Kyoto National Museum.** One of Japan's best collections of Buddhist and decorative art, near Sanjusangendo. 2–3 hours. | Bus to Sanjusangendo-mae, 15 min from the center. ¥700, special exhibitions extra |
| 15:00 | **[Tea ceremony](/blog/culture/tea-ceremony).** Seated, sheltered and calm. 45–90 min. Book ahead. | Venues across central Kyoto. ¥2,000–¥5,000 |
| 16:30 | **[Sake tasting in Fushimi](/blog/food/sake-kyoto).** The Gekkeikan museum and Kizakura both have indoor exhibits and tastings. 60–90 min. Check closing times before you go. | Kintetsu to Momoyama-Goryomae or Keihan to Fushimi-Momoyama, about 20 min. ¥500–¥1,000 |

**Another option: the Kyoto International Manga Museum.** A former elementary school with over 300,000 volumes to read on the spot. ¥900, all ages, near Karasuma-Oike subway station. Good for hours.

## Rain Basics

- **Umbrellas:** shake yours off outside and use the stand or the plastic sleeve at the door. Carrying a dripping umbrella through a shop or museum is rude.
- **Taxis:** harder to hail in the rain. Use an app such as GO or DiDi, ask your hotel to call one, or go to the stands at Kyoto Station and Shijo-Kawaramachi.
- **Photos:** keep the camera out. Overcast light is soft and even, and wet stone reflects the lanterns.
