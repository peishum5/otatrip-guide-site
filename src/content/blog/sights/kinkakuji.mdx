Kinkaku-ji is one gold-covered pavilion seen from a fixed one-way path. That makes it the quickest major sight in Kyoto and one of the most crowded, so when you arrive matters more than anything else.

## The Short Version

- **Be at the gate before 9am.** There is no early access, and the first hour is the quietest the garden gets all day.
- **Avoid noon to 3pm.** That is when a clear photo is hardest to get.
- **Entry is ¥500**, paid at the gate. No booking.
- **Allow 30–40 minutes.** You follow a set path and cannot go inside the pavilion.
- **Take the subway to Kitaoji, then a bus.** It is more reliable than riding the bus all the way from Kyoto Station.

## Getting There

- **Subway + bus (best):** Karasuma Line to Kitaoji Station, then Bus 204 or 205 to Kinkakuji-michi, about 11 minutes on the bus. It saves 10–15 minutes over the direct bus and avoids most traffic delays.
- **Direct bus:** City Bus 205 from Kyoto Station to Kinkakuji-michi, about 40 minutes and slow at rush hour. Bus 101, still listed in older guides, no longer runs.
- **Taxi:** worth it for a group or a tight schedule.

## What You See

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/kinkakuji-golden-exterior.jpg" alt="Kinkakuji golden pavilion three stories reflecting in Kyoko-chi pond" badge="GOLDEN PAVILION" caption="The pavilion from the main viewing area. Each of the three floors is built in a different style." />

- **Main viewpoint:** right after the entrance, across Kyoko-chi pond. This is the classic reflection shot and where everyone stops. Overcast days give a more even reflection than bright sun.
- **Upper garden:** the path loops up past Anmintaku Pond, with a stone pagoda and camellia trees. Usually quieter.
- **Tea near the exit:** by the Sekkatei Teahouse, matcha and a sweet cost ¥500 and beat the stalls outside the gate.

The pavilion was built in 1397 as a villa for the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu and became a Zen temple after his death. It burned down in 1950, and what you see is the 1955 reconstruction.

Two things to expect: the pavilion is smaller than photos suggest, and you cannot linger or leave the path. If it snows (5–10 days a year, mostly January and February), go early. Gold on white is the rarest view in Kyoto.

<TipBox type="warning" title="NO EARLY ACCESS">
Unlike some Kyoto temples, Kinkaku-ji does not open before 9am, so a dawn visit is not possible. Arrive at the gate before 9am to be in the first group through.
</TipBox>

## Add Ryoan-ji and Ninna-ji

[Ryoan-ji](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ryoanji+Kyoto) and its famous 15-stone Zen garden are about a 20-minute walk southwest. Entry is ¥600. Ninna-ji is about 10 minutes further on foot. Start at Kinkaku-ji at 9am and the three make a compact morning in northern Kyoto.
