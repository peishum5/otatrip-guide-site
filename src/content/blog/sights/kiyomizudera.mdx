Kiyomizu-dera is the hillside temple with the huge wooden stage, and it opens at 6am, hours before the tour groups arrive. Go early, then leave on foot through the old lanes below it.

## The Short Version

- **Go between 6am and 8am.** Tour groups arrive after 9am, and 10am to 3pm is the worst.
- **Entry is ¥500** for adults, ¥200 for children.
- **Allow 60–90 minutes.** The complex is bigger than most visitors expect.
- **Leave downhill through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka**, not back down the lane you came up. Add 30–45 minutes.
- **Wear comfortable shoes.** The approach is a steep stone lane and the grounds have many stairs.

## Getting There

- **Bus:** City Bus 206 from Kyoto Station to Gojo-zaka, about 15–20 minutes, then 10–15 minutes uphill along Kiyomizuzaka lane. Buses 202 and 207 also stop at Gojo-zaka and are often less crowded. Bus 100, recommended in older guides, no longer runs.
- **Taxi:** about ¥900–¥1,200 from Kyoto Station. Taxis can drop you closer to the gate than the bus.
- **On foot:** the temple is the southern end of the [Higashiyama walk](/blog/neighborhoods/higashiyama) to Yasaka Shrine.

## What to See

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/kiyomizudera-stage-view.jpg" alt="Kiyomizudera wooden stage extending over forested hillside" badge="MAIN HALL" caption="The wooden stage reaches 13 metres out over the hillside, built without a single nail." />

- **Main hall and stage:** the temple was founded in 778 and the current buildings date to 1633. The stage is the centerpiece, and the hillside below fills with color in cherry blossom and autumn seasons.
- **Okuno-in:** walk past the main hall to this sub-temple and look back. This is the postcard view of the whole stage.
- **Otowa Waterfall:** three streams below the stage, each said to grant a different wish: long life, success in school, or luck in love. Drink from one, not all three. The queue is long at midday.
- **Jishu Shrine:** behind the main hall, free, and dedicated to the god of love. It closed for long-term repairs in 2022, so check that it has reopened. Walk between the two "love stones", 18 metres apart, with your eyes closed.
- **Tainai-meguri:** a pitch-black corridor under Zuigudo Hall that you feel your way through by a rope. ¥100 and worth it.

<TipBox type="warning" title="TAINAI-MEGURI IS TOTAL DARKNESS">
The passage under Zuigudo Hall is completely black and phone lights are not allowed. Hold the rope and move slowly. It lasts 3–4 minutes and is not a good idea if you are claustrophobic.
</TipBox>
