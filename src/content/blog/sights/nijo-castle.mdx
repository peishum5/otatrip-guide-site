Nijo Castle is where the Tokugawa shogunate began in 1603 and ended in 1867. Unlike most Japanese castles, the original palace interior survives, and you walk straight through it.

## The Short Version

- **Go at the 8:45am opening on a weekday.** Tour groups fill the palace corridors by midday.
- **Entry is ¥1,300**, palace included (¥400 for junior high and high school students, ¥300 for elementary school children). Buy at the gate, no booking. The separate Honmaru Palace costs ¥1,000 more and must be booked online in advance.
- **Allow 2–2.5 hours** for the palace and the gardens.
- **No photos inside the palace.** This is strictly enforced.
- **Take the Tozai subway line** to Nijo-jo-mae Station, one minute from the gate.

## Inside Ninomaru Palace

<ImageCaption src="/images/blog/inline/nijo-castle-outer-walls.jpg" alt="Stone walls and ornate Karamon gate of Nijo Castle in early morning light" badge="NIJO CASTLE" caption="The gilded Karamon gate is the formal entrance to Ninomaru Palace. Before 9am you have the approach nearly to yourself." />

Shoes come off at the entrance, and you walk through six connected buildings, from the outer waiting rooms to the shogun's own chambers.

- **Nightingale floors:** every board chirps under your weight, so nobody could cross the palace unheard. The sound is clearest in the corridors between rooms. Slow down and listen.
- **Kano school paintings:** tigers, pines and hawks in gold leaf on the sliding doors. The panels in the rooms are reproductions, and the originals are shown in turn in the castle gallery (¥100). The rooms get richer the closer you come to the shogun's rooms.
- **The Ohiroma:** the main audience hall, where the shogun sat on a raised floor above visiting lords. The last shogun announced the return of power to the emperor here in 1867.

<TipBox type="warning" title="NO PHOTOS INSIDE THE PALACE">
Photography is prohibited inside Ninomaru Palace and staff watch throughout. Put your camera and phone away before you enter.
</TipBox>

## The Gardens

Allow 30–45 minutes after the palace.

- **Ninomaru Garden:** designed by Kobori Enshu in 1626, with three islands in a central pond. The best view is from the palace corridors.
- **Seiryu-en Garden:** laid out in 1965 with stones, trees and building parts from the estate of a wealthy Kyoto merchant family.
- **The main tower:** lightning destroyed it in 1750 and it was never rebuilt. Only the stone base remains.
- **Spring:** about 400 plum and cherry trees draw big crowds. The evening illumination costs extra and sells out weeks ahead. Dates are usually announced in mid-February, so book on the official website then. Daytime entry never needs booking.

## Getting There

- **Subway:** Tozai Line to Nijo-jo-mae Station, a 1-minute walk from the gate. The simplest way.
- **Bus:** City Bus 9 or 50 from Kyoto Station to Nijo-jo-mae, about 20 minutes.
- **Bicycle:** the castle is in flat central Kyoto, an easy ride from the station area.
